## 1. On the Nature of Happiness（论幸福的本质）

**英文正文**

Happiness is one of those words that everyone understands until they are asked to define it. For some, it is a destination to be reached; for others, a journey to be savored. The ancient philosophers, wiser perhaps than we credit them, insisted that happiness is not something we stumble upon but something we practice. It grows not from the abundance of what we possess, but from the depth of what we appreciate. This is why the pursuit of more so often leads to less: in chasing what we lack, we overlook what we already have. To be happy, then, is less a matter of changing our circumstances than of changing our gaze. Gratitude, practiced daily, has a way of turning the ordinary into the extraordinary.

**中文译文**

幸福是那种人人都懂、直到被要求下定义时才说不清道不明的词。对有些人来说，它是待抵达的终点；对另一些人来说，它是值得品味的旅程。古代哲学家——也许比我们以为的更睿智——坚持认为，幸福不是我们偶然撞见的东西，而是我们修炼出来的东西。它的生长，不源于我们拥有的丰盛，而源于我们感恩的深度。正因如此，对"更多"的追逐往往导致"更少"：在追逐所缺之物时，我们忽略了已经拥有的一切。那么，要获得幸福，与其说是改变处境，不如说是改变目光。日日践行的感恩，自有一种把平凡化为非凡的魔力。

**重点词汇与短语**

- a destination to be reached / a journey to be savored（待抵达的终点 / 待品味的旅程）
- not something we stumble upon but something we practice（不是偶然撞见，而是修炼而成）
- the abundance of what we possess（我们拥有的丰盛）
- the depth of what we appreciate（我们感恩的深度）
- turn the ordinary into the extraordinary（化平凡为非凡）

## 2. The Illusion of Control（控制的幻觉）

**英文正文**

We like to believe that we are the authors of our own lives, and to a considerable extent we are. Yet this comforting belief rests, more often than we care to admit, on an illusion. We plan meticulously, only to have our plans undone by a chance meeting, a sudden illness, or a turn in the economy that no one foresaw. The Stoics, those masters of equanimity, drew a distinction that remains useful today: there are things within our control, and there are things beyond it, and wisdom lies in knowing the difference. To spend our energy fretting over what we cannot change is to squander the very power we do possess. Serenity, then, is not the absence of trouble, but the presence of perspective.

**中文译文**

我们喜欢相信自己是人生的作者，在相当程度上也确实如此。然而，这一令人安心的信念，比我们愿意承认的更多时候，建立在幻觉之上。我们精心计划，却往往因一次偶遇、一场突如其来的疾病，或无人预见的经济转向而计划落空。斯多葛派——那些泰然自若的大师——曾划出一条至今仍有益的界限：有些事情在我们掌控之中，有些事情则超出掌控，而智慧就在于懂得其中的区别。把精力耗费在为无法改变之事焦虑上，就是挥霍我们真正拥有的力量。那么，宁静并非没有烦扰，而是拥有洞察。

**重点词汇与短语**

- the authors of our own lives（自己人生的作者）
- rest on an illusion（建立在幻觉之上）
- a chance meeting（一次偶遇）
- draw a distinction（划出界限）
- fret over what we cannot change（为无法改变之事焦虑）
- the presence of perspective（拥有洞察）

## 3. The Value of Solitude（独处的价值）

**英文正文**

In a culture that celebrates connection, solitude has acquired an undeserved reputation as something to be feared. We fill every idle moment with messages and notifications, as if silence were a vacuum that must be filled. Yet solitude, properly understood, is not loneliness; it is the condition in which we finally hear ourselves think. It was in solitude that great works were conceived and great questions first posed. To be comfortable alone is to be free, for it means our contentment does not depend on the approval or presence of others. The person who cannot bear an hour in their own company is, in a profound sense, a stranger to themselves. We would do well, then, to reclaim solitude not as an absence, but as a gift.

**中文译文**

在一个崇尚连接的文化里，独处蒙受了一种不应有的恶名，仿佛它令人恐惧。我们用消息和通知填满每一个空闲的瞬间，仿佛沉默是一个必须被填满的真空。然而，正确理解的独处并非孤独；它是一种我们终于能听见自己思考的状态。伟大的作品正是在独处中构思，伟大的问题正是在独处中首次提出。能安然独处，便是自由，因为它意味着我们的满足不依赖于他人的认可或在场。一个无法忍受与自己独处一小时的人，从深刻的意义上说，是自我的陌生人。那么，我们最好重新把独处视为一份礼物，而非一种缺失。

**重点词汇与短语**

- an undeserved reputation（不应有的名声）
- a vacuum that must be filled（必须被填满的真空）
- hear ourselves think（听见自己思考）
- depend on the approval of others（依赖他人的认可）
- a stranger to themselves（自我的陌生人）
- reclaim solitude as a gift（把独处重新视为礼物）

## 4. Language and Thought（语言与思维）

**英文正文**

The relationship between language and thought has long fascinated philosophers and scientists alike. Do we think in words, or do we merely clothe pre-existing thoughts in words after the fact? The question is more than academic, for the answer shapes how we understand the very limits of the mind. Some argue that the languages we speak subtly influence how we perceive color, time and space. Others contend that thought is largely universal, and that language is but a window through which we glimpse it. Wherever the truth lies, one thing seems certain: a richer vocabulary does not merely describe a richer inner life; it helps create it. To learn a word is to acquire a tool for noticing what previously escaped us.

**中文译文**

语言与思维之间的关系，长期以来既吸引着哲学家，也吸引着科学家。我们是用词语思考，还是仅仅在事后给早已存在的思想披上词语的外衣？这个问题并非纯学术性的，因为它的答案塑造了我们如何理解心灵的极限。有人主张，我们所讲的语言会潜移默化地影响我们感知颜色、时间和空间的方式。另一些人则认为，思维在很大程度上是普遍的，语言不过是让我们窥见它的一扇窗。无论真相何在，有一点似乎可以肯定：更丰富的词汇不只是描述更丰富的内心世界，它本身就有助于创造这个世界。学会一个词，就是获得一件工具，用以察觉从前被我们忽略的东西。

**重点词汇与短语**

- fascinate...alike（同样吸引着……）
- clothe thoughts in words（给思想披上词语的外衣）
- more than academic（并非纯学术性的）
- subtly influence（潜移默化地影响）
- a window through which we glimpse it（窥见它的一扇窗）
- acquire a tool for noticing（获得一件察觉的工具）

## 5. The Art of Persuasion（说服的艺术）

**英文正文**

Persuasion is among the most misunderstood of human arts. Most people imagine it as a battle of arguments, a contest in which the loudest voice or the cleverest logic prevails. But the truth is subtler. People are rarely persuaded by reasons alone; they are moved by trust, by shared values, and by the quiet sense that the speaker genuinely understands their concerns. This is why the most persuasive people listen more than they speak, and why they frame their arguments in the language of the listener rather than their own. Rhetoric, at its highest, is not manipulation but translation: the effort to render one's ideas intelligible and appealing to another mind. To persuade, one must first be willing to be persuaded, for dialogue is a dance in which no one leads alone.

**中文译文**

说服是人类最常被误解的技艺之一。大多数人把它想象成一场论据之战、一场最响亮的声音或最巧妙的逻辑获胜的较量。但真相更为微妙。人们很少仅凭道理就被说服；打动他们的是信任、共同的价值观，以及一种细微的感受——说话者真正理解他们的关切。正因如此，最会说服的人听得多、说得少，也正因如此，他们用听者的语言而非自己的语言来组织论点。修辞在其最高境界并非操纵，而是翻译：努力让自己的想法能被另一个头脑理解并认同。要说服他人，自己必须先愿意被说服，因为对话是一场没有人能独自领舞的双人舞。

**重点词汇与短语**

- among the most misunderstood of human arts（人类最常被误解的技艺之一）
- prevail（获胜；占上风）
- be moved by trust（被信任打动）
- frame an argument（组织论点）
- rhetoric is not manipulation but translation（修辞不是操纵而是翻译）
- a dance in which no one leads alone（无人独自领舞的舞蹈）
